# Publicity Plan — GetWorld (v1.0 alpha)

The goal at this stage is not sales. It is **wishlists + a community + a demo**,
because that is what converts into a launch later.

---

## The funnel (one sentence)

> One destination (Steam wishlist) + one discovery engine (YouTube) + one
> community (Reddit), cross-posted free, with a demo.

Everything below is the concrete version of that.

---

## Priority order

1. **Steam page with a wishlist button — now, even in alpha.**
   This is the single highest-ROI move in indie PC marketing. Everything else
   points here. "Coming soon" + wishlist is enough to start.

2. **A playable demo (the alpha IS the demo).**
   For a systems game the loop sells itself if people touch it. A free alpha
   demo converts better than any trailer.

3. **Shorts / TikTok / Reels as the discovery funnel → the Steam page.**
   One vertical video, cross-posted to all three. Not three separate efforts.

4. **Long-form devlogs on YouTube.**
   Build-in-public builds trust for a systems-heavy game. Topics are the
   systems coming together (see "devlog ideas" below).

5. **A one-page landing site (not a full website).**
   A static page (Carrd / Notion / itch.io) that hosts what Steam cannot:
   the demo download, the devlog, and an email signup.

---

## The one-line hook

Every video and the landing page should open with a repeatable premise:

> "Sid Meier's Colonization, but you run the whole supply chain yourself —
> horses, trade routes, and a tech tree."

(Adjust wording as the game tightens, but keep ONE hook and reuse it.)

---

## Short-form content (what actually works for this genre)

Not generic gameplay clips — **the satisfying loop, compressed**. The audience
watches:

- Timelapses: "I landed on a coast and built a trading colony in 60 seconds."
- Logistics: "I set up a horse route that carries tobacco out and brings
  mushrooms back."
- Danger: "The natives raided my settlement while I was away."
- Automation payoff: "I automated iron ore → bars → armor."
- Progression reveals: "I finally unlocked the Army."
- The premise in the first 2 seconds of every video.

Cross-post the exact same vertical video to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and
Instagram Reels. Cost: two minutes per post.

---

## Devlog ideas (long-form)

- "Building the horse trade routes."
- "How the free market prices your goods."
- "The natives fight back."
- "Designing the research tree."
- "From alpha idea to playable loop."

One devlog every 1–2 weeks beats daily low-effort posts.

---

## Where the audience is

The target player is a fan of: Factorio, RimWorld, Settlers, Anno, and old
*Sid Meier's Colonization*.

Find them in:

- r/BaseBuildingGames
- r/indiegames
- r/IndieDev
- r/gamedev
- Discords of the games above

Participate, post devlogs, do not spam.

---

## The landing page (one page, nothing more)

Must contain:

1. The one-line hook.
2. A 5–10 second gameplay loop / GIF.
3. 2–3 screenshots (research tree, settlement, defense).
4. Two big buttons: **Wishlist on Steam** and **Play the alpha demo**.
5. Links: devlog, Discord, email signup (the one thing Steam does not give you).

It is a front door to Steam, not a replacement for it.

---

## What to NOT do yet (timing-gated, save for launch-adjacent)

- **Press / YouTuber / streamer keys** — high value, but only once the demo
  shows well. Sending a rough alpha is wasted.
- **Steam Next Fest + indie showcases** — a big one, but it requires the demo
  and Steam page to exist first, so it comes later.
- **Paid ads** — almost never worth it for an indie at alpha; bad
  cost-per-wishlist.
- **Twitter/X** — dev-to-dev networking, not buyers; casual or skip.

---

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Splitting across five channels.** A solo dev who tries five channels does
  all of them badly. Steam + YouTube + Reddit, cross-posted, is enough.
- **"Looking into other methods" as procrastination.** The best marketing is a
  finished game + a demo + consistent content in one place.

---

## Naming flag

"GetWorld" reads as a tech/utility word, not a colonization game. The marketing
name (not necessarily the code) should be evocative — "New World", "Colonize
the Coast", "Settle", "Westward", etc. Worth deciding before the Steam page
goes up, because renaming later costs momentum.
